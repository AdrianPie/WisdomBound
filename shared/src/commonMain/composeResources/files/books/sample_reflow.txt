The Lantern Keeper

Chapter 1. The Cliff Road

The storm had passed before dawn, and the road along the cliff was littered
with branches. Mira walked it slowly, counting the broken ones. Her father had
taught her to read weather in wreckage: thin twigs meant wind, thick limbs meant
something worse. This morning the limbs were thick.

She had kept the lantern burning for nine years. Nine winters of climbing the
narrow stair, nine springs of scraping salt from the glass. Sailors she would
never meet steered by a light she tended alone, and that had always seemed to
her a fair arrangement.

Below the cliff, the sea was still angry. It threw itself at the rocks without
any hope of moving them, which struck Mira as a reasonable way to spend a life.

Chapter 2. What the Tide Left

The boat was small and painted a green that had mostly surrendered to the water.
It lay on its side in the shallows, one oar still lashed to the bench, the other
gone entirely. Mira climbed down to it because there was nobody else to climb
down, and because she had learned long ago that waiting for someone else is a
way of choosing not to know.

There was no one aboard. There was, instead, a wooden box wedged beneath the
bench, sealed with wax, and heavy in a way that suggested paper rather than
gold. She carried it up the cliff without opening it, which took the better part
of an hour and most of her patience.

That night she set the box on the table beneath the lantern and looked at it for
a long while. The light turned above her, steady as a heartbeat, throwing its
slow circle out across the water. Somewhere out there a ship was reading it.

She broke the seal.

Chapter 3. The Letters

Inside were letters, hundreds of them, each folded and numbered but none of them
addressed. The handwriting was the same throughout, growing smaller toward the
later numbers, as though the writer had begun to worry about running out of
paper. Mira read the first one twice before she understood what she was holding.

They were not letters to anyone. They were letters to a lighthouse.
